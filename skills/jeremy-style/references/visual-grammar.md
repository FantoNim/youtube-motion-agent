# Visual grammar: observed versus proposed

Use the evidence IDs in source-evidence.md for source observations. All numerical tokens below are deliberately chosen adaptation defaults, not recovered author settings.

## Core identity

Build small theatrical worlds around a readable emotional situation. The source uses large spherical pale heads, tiny hoodie/dress bodies, tubular limbs, round hands and feet; minimal graphic eyes, brows and mouths do most of the acting (E01, E04, E10). Sets are recognizable, economical school/social locations rather than photoreal interiors. A soft purple cinematic atmosphere ties bedroom, gym, cafeteria/classroom, corridor, stadium/concert and street together. Gold serif information labels and pink light paths turn otherwise invisible relationships into visible mechanics.

Preserve these relationships rather than copying source cast, story, statistics or exact sets. An adult educational adaptation can use office/workshop scenes, provided the physical staging, emotional acting and explanatory inserts remain coherent.

## Character construction and acting

Observed: oversized smooth heads; narrow necks; blocky rounded clothing; white-ish capsule legs and mitten/sphere hands; cap/hair silhouette, clothing color and minimal accessories identify cast. The blue-hoodie protagonist recurs in many contexts; a green cap/hoodie companion remains distinguishable (E04, E09). Expressions range from half-lidded confidence to surprise, tired/closed eyes and concern; not every character smiles. Faces are graphic marks on rounded volume, not detailed skin anatomy.

Adaptation: start with a head diameter about 35–42% of total character height, body about 25–30%, and thin limbs. These ratios are proposed, not measured. Make a model sheet before animation. Keep one identifier per supporting character plus optional short introduction label; use color plus silhouette, not color alone. Maintain fixed head/body proportions across shots. Rig mouth, brows and eyes independently from head turns. Avoid continuous bobbing as a substitute for acting. Use distinct thinking, pointing, apprehensive, neutral and delighted poses when the narration warrants them. For this user's speaking avatar use clean tooth-free mouths and genuinely distinct hand/arm poses, preserving the approved master.

## Material and edge treatment

Observed: soft, smooth, toy/plastic-like shading on heads and clothes, broad highlights, rounded silhouettes and readable contact shadows. Faces stay dark and graphic. Environmental geometry is relatively simple and has broad colored surfaces; the mirror ball at 8s is an intentional reflective exception. The source does not establish physically measured roughness, shaders, texture sizes or exact render engine.

Adaptation: use non-metallic, moderately rough materials for cast; broad soft highlights, no skin pores or wet glossy eyes. Try roughness 0.45–0.7 and metalness 0 as starting points, then judge the encoded frame. Use a subtly different clothing roughness rather than noisy fabric. Reserve emissive material for pink paths/hearts, signs and stage screens. Keep emission from whitening the entire character. A contact shadow must anchor feet, desk props and hands. Avoid ink outlines on every 3D edge; use dark face marks and selective flat crowd linework instead.

## Palette and lighting

Observed roles: dark indigo/purple shadows and stage; lavender rim/key spill; warm cream/gold headlines and labels; pink/magenta relationship beams and counters; blue, green, orange, yellow and pink garments distinguish cast. School walls/lockers and sunset windows provide other local colors. This is not an exclusively purple monochrome video. Night/concert frames can be very dark (E11); do not blindly reproduce crushed detail.

Proposed project tokens: stage #1B142C; deep shadow #100D1E; purple fill #594073; cream head #EEE7E4; warm type #F1D9A0; relationship accent #F06BC0; blue garment #57AFD0; green garment #59A34A. These are role-matched starting colors, NOT sampled exact source swatches. Replace garment colors with approved branding selectively; keep neutral reading contrast.

Use a large warm key roughly front/side, a weak lavender fill and a restrained cool/purple rim. Place practical lights in the set when meaningful; let sunset windows explain warm edges. Separate face from background before adding bloom. Keep a consistent light direction within a dialogue exchange. Light the face for expression first; atmosphere second. Do not bake text into an emissive surface if it destroys legibility.

## Camera and spatial layout

Observed: extreme face closeups, over-the-shoulder views, medium two-shots, high corridor views, wide venue establishing shots, and low-angle scale reveals alternate by purpose (E01, E04, E09–E11). Foreground heads/rails/doorframes occlude subjects; perspective rows and large-scale venue views provide strong 3D cues. Focus falloff separates people from background crowds. A 1.25→1.5s view change and a 437.5→437.75s cut are resolved by dense samples; exact lens/easing is unknown.

Adaptation: choose a fixed 35–55mm-equivalent-ish perspective starting point for most human-scale shots; treat 28–35mm as an establishing option, not a universal lens. Use a camera-space framing test rather than assuming those numbers reproduce the source. Aim at the eye line. Establish the geography before closeups. Use foreground occluders sparingly so the face and meaningful prop stay visible. Keep UI/text overlays screen-space, outside shallow depth-of-field. Move a camera only to reveal information, change emotional proximity or expose scale. Do not continuously orbit the speaker.

## Typography, overlays and composition

Observed: uppercase gold/cream serif headings with extra spacing on purple explanation boards; prominent numeric pills/counters; short labels above miniatures; occasional white comic speech bubbles; in-world phone/TV and notices have different UI type. There is no evidence for a precise commercial font identity. The 66.392s frame has a three-line question with a larger last line over a small protagonist; 632–637s closes with gold question, choice icons and then a cast/engagement layout (E03, E12).

Adaptation: choose a licensed local bold display serif (for example a verified installed equivalent of a classical inscriptional/book serif); do not claim it is the source font. Use a clean sans for UI and dense small labels. For 1920x1080 start with 64–92px display headings, 30–44px labels and no more than 3 headline lines; test actual longest copy at delivery size. Keep roughly 6–8% outer composition margins as a starting point. Put one leading visual action below/alongside the headline. Use a pill for a changing quantity, a line for a relationship, a diagram for a comparison. Keep counts/data truthful; numbers seen in the reference are story evidence, not statistics to reuse. Text is selective explanation, not full verbatim kinetic subtitles.

The source contains baked black letterbox bands inside 1920x1080. Preserve them only when the brief wants that cinematic treatment; otherwise compose natively to the deliverable ratio. Do not crop essential heads/type to imitate a letterbox. Vertical work requires re-blocking, not a blind center crop.
